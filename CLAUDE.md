# Wait Your Turn (formerly The Oldest Congress)

Sydney's data viz final (SJSU MDes). An interactive chamber: every seat is a member of Congress, drawn as a halftone portrait on a flat color chip. Filters light up matching seats and dim the rest.

## Files
- `chamber.html` is the built, self-contained page. Do not edit it by hand. Edit `src/template.html`, then run `python3 src/build.py`.
- `src/template.html` has all HTML, CSS and JS. `/*DATA*/` is replaced with JSON at build time (`data`, `ext`, `votes`, `pop`, `hist`, `tl`, `fimg`, `img`).
- `data/quiz_explainers.csv` holds each quiz vote's bill number, Congress.gov link, whether it became law, `quiz_order` (the 8 votes the Issues tab asks; blank = not asked), a plain `question` headline, and a plain "What it does" line paraphrased from the Congressional Research Service (CRS) summary (fetched from GovInfo BILLSUM, since Congress.gov blocks scripts). `src/build.py` merges it, plus the House/Senate tallies from `quiz_vote_candidates.csv`, into `votes`.
- `data/generation_timeline.csv` holds the events for "What was happening when they were young": generation index (0 = Gen Z ... 4 = Silent, same as `GEN`), `born` or `20`, year, text, source name and URL. Every row needs a source; links were checked to load on 2026-10-27.
- `data/fact_images.csv` picks a Wikimedia Commons photo per event (key `gen-when-year`, crop `c` center or `t` top). `pipeline/build_fact_images.py` downloads them to `data/raw/facts/`, checks the license is free (public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA), and writes 56x56 halftone grids plus credits to `data/fact_img.json` (`fimg` in the build). Uses curl and macOS `sips`. Copyrighted pop culture (Superman, Barbie, Star Wars, Friends, Jurassic Park, TikTok) and two events with no free photo (Brown v. Board, 26th Amendment) get a year tile instead.
- `data/` has the inputs. `data/img.json` holds 24x30 luminance grids per member (base64), drawn as halftone on canvas. `data/age_by_congress.csv` is the 1st-119th history table. `data/raw/` holds the congress-legislators JSON (current and historical) it is built from.
- `pipeline/build_history.py` rebuilds `data/age_by_congress.csv`. Run it from the project root, then `python3 src/build.py`.
- The other `pipeline/` scripts built the earlier tables. They use absolute /home/claude paths from the old environment and are reference only.
- `analysis/`: `analyze.py` (stdlib) writes `results.json` with every number in the data analysis. `charts.py` draws its SVG charts. `build_sheets.py` writes the design system and assets sheets. `build_report.py` writes `proposal/data_analysis.html`, printed to the Data Analysis PDF with headless Chrome. See HANDOFF.md.
- `proposal/`: the class documents (updated proposal, data analysis) and `img/` with screenshots and sketches. Documents use a plain Google Docs style: Archivo only, regular or semi-bold, black text, simple bordered tables.
- Local preview: `.claude/launch.json` serves the folder at http://localhost:8765/chamber.html. Headless Chrome will not go narrower than about 500px, so check phone widths in the browser pane instead.

## Page structure
- Five views from the header nav: **Chamber**, **Play**, **How Congress works**, **About the data**, **Sources**. `showView()` switches them.
- **Header stats** follow the scrubber: Congress median age and US median age for that year. US median exists only for 2020-2025, so 2017 and 2019 show "—" with a note.
- **Chamber view**, top to bottom:
  - one big headline (`#take`): members 70+ that year, with "Up/Down from N in 2017" in italic serif;
  - one panel (`.stagebar`) holding the House/Senate/Both view, the generation chips and the party chips (All/Democrat/Republican/Independent, `S.party`, works for every Congress; seats are never colored by party), plus the Key (legend and four short icon rows, opening inside the panel). Clicking a generation chip filters the chamber and opens the generation modal (`<dialog id="gmodal">`): "Did you know?" cards, one event at a time, each with a halftone photo (or a year tile when no free photo exists), the year, the event, what ages that generation's members turned that year, and the source. "See them in the chamber" closes it and keeps the filter;
  - the chamber with the lit count in the middle;
  - the 115th-119th scrubber, with one mono caption under it (`#caption`): change in 70+ and new members since the previous Congress, or a "drag the timeline" hint on the 115th (eased, snaps to whole Congresses; only the knob is styled).
  - The story lines always describe the whole chamber and ignore filters.
- **Sidebar** (right rail, bottom sheet on phones) with three tools: Your age, Who fits, Issues. The drawer header is a tool picker (`#toolpick` opens `#toolmenu`, one item per tool with a one-line description and a "filtering" badge when it is active), so new tools are added as menu items, not tabs. Issues is "How would you have voted?": 8 votes, one card at a time (a plain question, What it does, Congress.gov link, Yes/No/Skip, what happened), numbered dots to jump. The chamber dims from the first answer, and a readout gives how many members matched and their median age. Each tool is one heading, one hint line and a widget box (`.wbox`): Your age shows the age big plus a younger / within / older stacked bar; Who fits puts each trait group in its own panel; Issues uses a segmented control (`#thr`, Every vote / All but 1 / All but 2) and a readout box with the big match count and median age. Only the open tool filters. The other tools keep their inputs but stand down until reopened.
- **Play view** ("Wait your turn", in progress since Nov 17): a story game in `src/game.html`, `src/game.css` and `src/game.js`, which `build.py` inlines at `<!--GAME_HTML-->`, `/*GAME_CSS*/` and `/*GAME_JS*/`. Flow: title, character creator, dialogue with Quorum (a made-up Capitol pigeon), walk to the chamber, pick any 2017 member's seat, then the four real elections (2018-2024) one at a time, each ending in a "Still there" or "Seat open" card, then a result poster. It follows the person, not the district, and never says the player would have won. `build.py` writes `game` (per 115th member: first year in Congress, seat label, first Congress gone from that chamber, successor only when a brand-new member holds the same seat number, moved-chamber flag). No invented odds; the character, pigeon and dialogue are the only made-up parts.
  - Look (rebuilt Nov 17): cross stitch over soft photos, the direction Sydney chose after rejecting code-drawn vectors and then riso. Everything on the stage is a chart of X stitches on one canvas grid (`#gcv`): full crosses and half stitches, thread white `#f7f0df` plus the generation colors, small spaced caps for UI text. Not pixel art (she finds that too digital). She prefers the detailed Capitol (half stitches for the dark lines), not a solid silhouette, and a quick, smooth sew-in (about 1.5s, eased).

## Design decisions (Sydney's)
- White, minimal page. Bold type (Archivo, Instrument Serif italic, IBM Plex Mono). Cut-paper collage and halftone assets sit on top of the white. Single light look.
- The chamber is the main visual, centered in the page. Supporting tools live in the sidebar; extra text lives in About and Sources.
- Sydney wants less text, fewer levels of hierarchy and more white space. Every section is its own soft panel (`.block`/`.hblock`, `--panel`), with white cards inside. One title plus one short line per box; long method notes go in "About this chart" / "Details" toggles (`details.more`). Prefer interactive widgets over paragraphs. Never put four stats in a row (or any row of identical big-number tiles); Sydney calls it an overused AI-design tell. Show one number at a time with a picker, or use rows, a checklist or a chart. The generation timeline in About shows one generation at a time (`#gtlbar`).
- Halftone dots are always black, never white or inverted.
- Generation colors (`GC`) avoid red and blue so they don't read as party: Gen Z mustard `#c9ad3b`, Millennial green `#45a86f`, Gen X pink `#e98dca`, Boomer purple `#9e63d6`, Silent brown `#ae6c18`. Checked with the dataviz palette validator (light, all pairs): passes, worst colorblind ΔE 8.5. Contrast vs white is under 3:1, so labels always sit beside the chips.
- References: halftone collage objects, flat vector crowds, bold type posters, tactile knobs.

## Data rules (do not break)
- Missing means unknown, never "no". Race labels come from Wikipedia lists, not self-report; unlisted is not White. Religion is Pew/CQ self-report. Immigrant counts are minimums.
- How unknowns show in Who fits:
  - A known non-match dims the seat.
  - A missing religion or immigrant value is unknown: faded seat with a dark dashed outline (`na`).
  - Race: a member on a different race list dims. A member on no list is unknown (`nq`), drawn like a dim seat so the chamber stays readable, but counted in "· N unknown" and explained in the panel note.
- No filter may be based on how a portrait looks.
- Issue quiz (8 votes, picked for being easy to explain and covering different topics):
  - filters live from the first answer;
  - agreement is over votes both people cast; absences are excluded;
  - a member must have cast min(your answers, 3) of them, else `na`;
  - "every vote" (default), "all but one" (one difference allowed from 3 compared votes) or "all but two" (two allowed from 5);
  - no ranking; neutral Yes/No wording;
  - say which votes split by party (overdraft fees, Canada tariffs, Iran and Arctic oil did; fentanyl, detention, clean cars and stablecoins drew dozens of crossovers).
- Seat layout is by DW-NOMINATE order and is not real seating. Say so on the page.
- Age in the chamber (115th-119th) is measured on January 3 of the Congress's first year. The age field is rounded to 0.1 (so Frost shows 28.0); whole-year ages (`yrs`, the last field in each `data` row) are computed in `build.py` from the birthday and are what the How Congress works view uses.
- History chart rules (`pipeline/build_history.py`):
  - Age is measured on the day each Congress first convened: the most common House term start between its legal start (March 4 through 1933, January 3 from 1935) and the next. For the 115th-119th this is January 3, matching the chamber.
  - Counts voting members whose term covers that day. Territorial delegates are left out until statehood.
  - Medians use known birth dates only; early Congresses are missing up to a third.
  - Older records list a vacancy-filler for the whole Congress, so some seats count two people (up to 555 listed). Removing them changes the median by at most 1.5 years. The page says this.
  - Counts before 1913 are not directly comparable because Congress grew from 89 to 535 members.
