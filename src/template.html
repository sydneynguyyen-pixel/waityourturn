    About this chart

    Each column is one Congress, measured on the day it first convened (January 3 for the 115th to 119th, the Congresses in the chamber, shaded). Voting members only; territorial delegates are left out until statehood. Birth dates are missing for up to a third of members in the earliest Congresses, so those medians use known dates only. In older records a member who filled a vacancy is listed for the whole Congress, so a few seats count two people. Congress grew from 89 to 535 voting members, so counts before 1913 are not directly comparable.

        Lit and dim

        Lit seats match the view, generation and tool you have open. Dim seats do not. Dashed ovals are empty seats. A seat with a dashed outline is unknown for your filter: not on the source list for a trait, or too few shared votes to compare in the quiz. Unknown is never counted as a no.         What the trait labels mean

        Blank means the sources don't say. It never means "no."

        Details

        Race and ethnicity labels come from Wikipedia lists of members, not from self-identification, and a member who is not listed is unknown, not White.         How would you vote?

        How the issue quiz matches

        8 real votes. Some split by party, so matches partly sort by party.

        Details

        The issue quiz uses 8 roll-call votes from the 119th Congress, picked because they are easy to explain and cover different topics. Four split mostly by party, so matches partly sort by party. That is a property of the votes, not a recommendation. Votes are matched to the same issue in each chamber, which sometimes meant two different but parallel resolutions.

        How the numbers were made

        Who is counted
        Every member seated on January 3 of each Congress, from the 115th (2017) to the 119th (2025). The roster is from unitedstates/congress-legislators.
        Age
        Measured on January 3 of that Congress's first year. The median US age is from Census estimates and exists only for 2020 to 2025.
        Generations
        Birth years follow Pew Research Center.
        Seat order
        Left to right by voting record (Voteview DW-NOMINATE). It is not the real seating plan.
        Unknown is not no
        A missing religion or immigrant value is shown as unknown, never as a no. Race labels come from Wikipedia lists, not from members themselves, and a member on no list is not counted as White. Immigrant counts are minimums.
        Since 1789
        Age is measured on the day each Congress first convened. Voting members only. Birth dates are missing for up to a third of the earliest members, and Congress grew from 89 to 535 members, so early counts are not directly comparable.
        The 8 votes
        Agreement is counted only over votes both you and a member cast. Missed votes are skipped. Matches are never ranked.
        The game
        Members, ages and elections are real. The character, the pigeon and the dialogue are made up. It never gives odds.
        Known gaps
        Six members have no portrait. The chamber, portraits and traits cover 2017 to 2025 only.

            Match
            How matching works

            Four of these votes split mostly by party (overdraft fees, Canada tariffs, Iran, Arctic oil), so matches partly sort by party. The other four drew dozens of members across party lines. Members stay lit when they voted the same way as you on the votes you answered. "All but one" allows one difference once you have 3 votes in common; "all but two" allows two once you have 5. Votes a member missed are skipped, never counted as disagreement; a member who missed too many to compare gets a dashed outline. Matches are not ranked. "What it does" is based on each bill's summary by the Congressional Research Service, Congress's nonpartisan research office.
