How the game works
The point
A seat in Congress opens only when the person in it leaves, and most members spent years building a résumé before they ran. The game shows both.
Why wait
Each House district has one seat. Anyone eligible can run against the member who holds it, but members who run again rarely lose, so in this game you wait for the seat to open. That part is a simplification.
What is real
The three seats and what happened to them. Maya waits for Michael C. Burgess's seat (TX-26), which opened at the 2024 election. Dan waits for Eddie Bernice Johnson's seat (TX-30), which opened at the 2022 election. Rosa waits for Harold Rogers's seat (KY-5), which Harold Rogers still held in 2025. The ages are real, measured on January 3 of each Congress. The characters, Quorum the pigeon and the dialogue are made up.
Points
Each choice is worth the share of the 535 seats in the 2025 Congress held by people with that background, rounded: 251 served in a state legislature (47 points), about 182 practiced law (34), at least 163 ran a business (30), at least 91 were congressional staff (17), 38 were mayors (7), 5 House members were union representatives (1), 3 were AmeriCorps volunteers (1). Source: CRS report R48535, from members' own profiles. The cards hide the points until you pick. Members overlap, so added-up points are a game score, not a share of anything.
The rule
You get in if your seat opens and you have 75 points. This is the game's rule, not a prediction. Nobody has measured how much any one step raises a real person's chance of reaching Congress.
Endings
"You got in": the seat opened and you had 75 points. "The door opened too early": the seat opened first. "Still closed": the member was still there after the 2024 election. Every ending says who really took the seat, if anyone new did.
75 or older
The row of characters at the top of the walk has one # for each member 75 or older on January 3 of each Congress: 32 in 2017 and 54 in 2025. The game says nothing about any member's ability; it only counts ages and years in office.
