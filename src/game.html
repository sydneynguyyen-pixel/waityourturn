How the game works
What is real
Every member, age and election. The rosters are everyone seated on January 3 of 2017, 2019, 2021, 2023 and 2025. Your character, Quorum the pigeon and the dialogue are made up.
Your age
Everyone starts at 25 in 2017, the youngest the House allows, so you are old enough for a House seat the whole game. You are 33 by 2025. The Senate requires 30, which you reach in 2022. At each election the game uses your age at the next swearing-in.
Still there
After each election the game checks whether the member you picked is still in the same chamber in the new Congress. If they are, you keep walking.
Seat open
The member is no longer in that chamber: they retired, lost, died, resigned or moved to the other chamber. The game follows the person, not the district, because district lines were redrawn along the way. It names a successor only when a brand-new member holds the same seat.
Still waiting
The member was still there after all four elections, 2018 to 2024.
75 or older
The row of characters at the top of the walk has one # for each member 75 or older; the brighter ones were added since 2017. The game counts members who were 75 or older on January 3 of each Congress: 32 in 2017 and 54 in 2025. The seat picker lights them, but you can pick anyone. The Chamber page headline uses 70 or older, so its numbers are larger. The game says nothing about any member's ability; it only counts ages and years in office.
Who took the seats
The picture at the end has one small figure for each brand-new member who took a seat that a 2017 member left, 2019 to 2025, grouped by their age on the day they were sworn in: under 35, 35 to 49, 50 or older. Seats with no brand-new member in the same seat afterward are left out. Your group is the one that holds your age when your seat opened, or in 2025 if it never did.
When they were your age
After you pick a seat, Quorum names one event from the generation timeline on the Chamber page (Generations): the one closest to the year that member turned 25, your age at the start. The age shown is the age they turned that year. Each event's source is listed under About this site.
New members
"New" means not in Congress two years earlier. Their median age is the middle value of their ages on January 3.
Your choices
What you do between elections is a made-up example and does not change any ending. The number beside each one is real: how many people in the 2025 Congress list the same thing in their own profiles, from the Congressional Research Service, Membership of the 119th Congress: A Profile (R48535, updated August 4, 2025). The numbers are not added up, because one member can be counted in several.
Fast forward
Arithmetic only. First election that could seat you: the first even-year election after which you would be 25 (House) or 30 (Senate) at swearing-in. "You reach the median age": the year you turn the 2025 median age, rounded up. The lit seats are members born before 1992, the year your character was born.
What it never does
It never gives odds and never says you would have won. Nobody has measured how much any one step raises a person's chance of reaching Congress, so the game does not make up a number for it.
